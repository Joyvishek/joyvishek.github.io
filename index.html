01 · Who I am

Product engineer first, AI architect by demand

I lead a product module day-to-day and, alongside it, design and build the AI layer the product runs on — the orchestration, the tool boundaries, and the retrieval that make an LLM trustworthy enough to take real actions.

My work sits at the intersection of three things: enterprise full-stack delivery (the systems that already work and can't break), applied AI (LLM tool-calling, RAG, computer vision), and team leadership (turning an architecture into something 12–15 engineers can actually ship).

Operating principle

AI should never be a demo layer bolted onto a product. It needs auth context, typed contracts, retries, graceful degradation, and domain services underneath it — the same rigor as any other production system.

Why it holds up under change

Each tool service is independently deployable and fails without taking the others down — the orchestrator treats an unreachable service as a structured error, not a crash. That isolation is what let the platform swap and re-architect individual services over time without ever pausing the product it powers.
