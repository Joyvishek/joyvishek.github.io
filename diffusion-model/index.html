The Diffusion Encyclopedia

A rigorous, academic, and exhaustive exposition of Diffusion Models, from foundational ELBO derivations to Score-Based SDEs and Diffusion Transformers.

The transition from discriminative modeling (classifying existing information) to generative modeling (synthesizing novel information) represents a profound paradigm shift in artificial intelligence. While Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) paved the way, the contemporary revolution in high-fidelity synthesis—ranging from photorealistic imagery to novel molecular structures—is driven almost entirely by the Diffusion Model.

This text serves as a definitive architectural and mathematical reference. It systematically deconstructs the mechanisms of generative diffusion: establishing the probabilistic foundations, proving the governing theorems, detailing the neural network architectures, and examining the continuous-time and flow-matching formulations that define the current state of the art.

Chapter 01

Non-Equilibrium Thermodynamics

Diffusion models are a class of latent variable models parameterized by Markov chains, deeply inspired by non-equilibrium thermodynamics. The conceptual foundation rests on the fact that any complex data distribution $p_{data}(x)$ can be systematically degraded into a simple, tractable distribution (typically an isotropic Gaussian) via a gradual diffusion process, akin to molecules of a solvent distributing themselves evenly over time.

In physics, this is modeled by Langevin dynamics. If we consider a data point $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$, the destruction of its information over continuous time $t \in [0, T]$ is a process of increasing entropy. The true generative breakthrough of the 2020s was recognizing that if this forward destruction process is modeled as a sequence of infinitesimally small Gaussian steps, the reverse process must also take the form of a Gaussian distribution.

By training a neural network to estimate the parameters of this reverse Gaussian, we essentially train a computational system to act as Maxwell's Demon—reversing the arrow of entropy to reconstruct highly structured data distributions from pure thermal noise.

Chapter 02

The Markovian Forward Process

Let $x_0 \sim q(x_0)$ represent a sample from our true data distribution. We define the forward diffusion process as a Markov chain that adds Gaussian noise over $T$ discrete timesteps, producing a sequence of latent variables $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_T$ possessing the exact same dimensionality as $x_0$.

The forward transition kernel is defined as:

$$ q(x_t \vert x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(x_t; \sqrt{1 - \beta_t} x_{t-1}, \beta_t \mathbf{I}) $$

Where $\beta_t \in (0, 1)$ is the variance schedule. A critical property of this formulation is that we can bypass iterative computation. By defining $\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t$ and $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^t \alpha_s$, we can leverage the reparameterization trick (since the sum of Gaussians is Gaussian) to sample $x_t$ directly from $x_0$:

$$ q(x_t \vert x_0) = \mathcal{N}(x_t; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x_0, (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t) \mathbf{I}) $$

This allows us to express any noisy state explicitly as:

$$ x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon, \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I}) $$

As $T \to \infty$, we design the schedule such that $\bar{\alpha}_T \to 0$. Thus, the marginal distribution $q(x_T)$ approaches an isotropic Gaussian distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$, erasing all information regarding the initial condition $x_0$.

Chapter 03

The Reverse Process & ELBO Derivation

The generative phase requires reversing the forward chain: we wish to sample $x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ and iteratively sample $q(x_{t-1} \vert x_t)$. However, calculating $q(x_{t-1} \vert x_t)$ directly requires evaluating the marginal distribution of all possible data, which is computationally intractable. Therefore, we approximate it using a parameterized neural network $p_\theta$:

$$ p_\theta(x_{t-1} \vert x_t) = \mathcal{N}(x_{t-1}; \mu_\theta(x_t, t), \Sigma_\theta(x_t, t)) $$

Deriving the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)

To optimize $p_\theta$, we maximize the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) of the log-likelihood of the data $\log p(x_0)$.

Theorem: ELBO Decomposition

The negative log-likelihood is bounded by the variational upper bound $L_{VLB}$:

$$ L_{VLB} = \mathbb{E}_q \left[ -\log \frac{p_\theta(x_{0:T})}{q(x_{1:T} \vert x_0)} \right] \ge -\mathbb{E}_q [\log p_\theta(x_0)] $$

By expanding the Markov chain probabilities and utilizing Bayes' theorem, Ho et al. (2020) demonstrated that $L_{VLB}$ can be elegantly decomposed into three distinct terms:

$$ L_{VLB} = L_T + \sum_{t>1} L_{t-1} + L_0 $$
  • $L_T = D_{KL}(q(x_T \vert x_0) \parallel p(x_T))$: This term asserts that the final forward step should match the prior standard Gaussian. Because our schedule $\beta_t$ is fixed, $L_T$ has no learnable parameters and is treated as a constant.
  • $L_0 = -\log p_\theta(x_0 \vert x_1)$: The reconstruction term for the final step of generation.
  • $L_{t-1} = D_{KL}(q(x_{t-1} \vert x_t, x_0) \parallel p_\theta(x_{t-1} \vert x_t))$: This is the workhorse of the loss function. It compares the network's predicted reverse step against the true posterior of the forward process, conditioned on $x_0$.

The Tractable Posterior and Reparameterization

A crucial mathematical revelation is that while $q(x_{t-1} \vert x_t)$ is intractable, the posterior conditioned on $x_0$ is tractable and strictly Gaussian:

$$ q(x_{t-1} \vert x_t, x_0) = \mathcal{N}(x_{t-1}; \tilde{\mu}_t(x_t, x_0), \tilde{\beta}_t \mathbf{I}) $$

Where the true mean is derived analytically as:

$$ \tilde{\mu}_t(x_t, x_0) = \frac{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}} \beta_t}{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} x_0 + \frac{\sqrt{\alpha_t}(1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t-1})}{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} x_t $$

Simplifying to Noise Prediction

Instead of predicting the mean $\tilde{\mu}_t$ directly, Ho et al. reparameterized $x_0$ in terms of $x_t$ and the injected noise $\epsilon$. By substituting this back into the loss term, they found the network simply needs to predict the noise $\epsilon$ that was added at step $t$:

$$ L_{simple}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})} \left[ \Vert \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \Vert^2 \right] $$

This $L_{simple}$ objective discards the complex weighting terms of the true ELBO, empirically resulting in vastly superior generative sample quality.

Chapter 04

Continuous-Time SDEs & Score Matching

While DDPMs formulate diffusion in discrete timesteps, Song et al. (2020) generalized this by allowing the number of steps $T \to \infty$, transforming the discrete Markov chain into a continuous-time Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE). This unifies Denoising Diffusion with Score-Based Generative Modeling.

The forward process is defined by an Ito SDE:

$$ dx = f(x, t)dt + g(t)dw $$

Where $f(x, t)$ is the drift coefficient (pulling the data deterministically), $g(t)$ is the diffusion coefficient (injecting noise), and $w$ is standard Brownian motion.

A profound result by Anderson (1982) dictates that the reverse-time process is also an SDE:

$$ dx = \left[ f(x, t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x) \right] dt + g(t) d\bar{w} $$

The only unknown in this reverse equation is $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$, which is the score function of the data distribution at time $t$. We train a neural network $s_\theta(x, t)$ to approximate this score via Score Matching techniques. Strikingly, predicting the noise $\epsilon$ in DDPM is mathematically equivalent to score matching via Tweedie's Formula:

$$ s_\theta(x_t, t) \approx -\frac{\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)}{\sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}} $$

SDE Classifications

  • Variance Exploding (VE-SDE): Adds noise without attenuating the original signal. The variance approaches infinity. (Equivalent to NCSN).
  • Variance Preserving (VP-SDE): Scales the signal down as noise is added, keeping the total variance strictly bounded at 1.0. (Equivalent to continuous DDPM).
  • Sub-VP SDE: A variant where variance is bounded even tighter, empirically avoiding high-variance bottlenecks during sampling.

Chapter 07

The U-Net Backbone

The workhorse function approximator $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)$ requires an architecture capable of processing dense spatial features while maintaining pixel-perfect structural alignment. The dominant choice has historically been the U-Net.

The U-Net operates through a spatial bottleneck:

  1. Encoder: A sequence of ResNet blocks and downsampling layers (e.g., strided convolutions or max pooling) that compress the spatial dimensions $H \times W$ while increasing the channel dimension $C$. This extracts global, semantic context.
  2. Bottleneck: The lowest resolution tier, containing massive channel depths, typically integrated with Multi-Head Self-Attention layers to calculate global spatial correlations.
  3. Decoder: Upsampling layers that reconstruct the spatial dimensions. Crucially, skip connections concatenate the high-resolution feature maps from the Encoder directly into the Decoder, rescuing fine-grained spatial details lost in the bottleneck.

Conditioning Injection (AdaGN)

The network must process the scalar timestep $t$. $t$ is projected into a high-dimensional vector using Sinusoidal Positional Embeddings and passed through a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). This temporal embedding is injected into *every* ResNet block via Adaptive Group Normalization (AdaGN).

$$ \text{AdaGN}(h, t) = y_s(t) \odot \text{GroupNorm}(h) + y_b(t) $$

Where $y_s$ and $y_b$ are scale and shift parameters derived from the timestep embedding. This allows time to physically alter the distribution of features throughout the entire network.

Chapter 08

Latent Space & Autoencoders (VAE)

Running a U-Net on high-resolution pixel space requires catastrophic computational complexity ($O(N^2)$ for attention layers). Rombach et al. solved this by introducing Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs), the architecture powering Stable Diffusion.

LDMs utilize a two-stage approach:

  1. Perceptual Compression (The VAE): A pre-trained Variational Autoencoder (VAE) compresses the pixel-space image $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ into a dense, lower-dimensional latent space $z \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w \times c}$, typically with a downsampling factor of $f=8$ (e.g., $1024 \to 128$).
  2. Semantic Diffusion: The Markovian forward and reverse diffusion process is executed *strictly* within this compressed latent space $z_t$. Because $128 \times 128$ requires $64\times$ fewer computations than $1024 \times 1024$, the model can allocate heavily localized self-attention mechanisms without exceeding VRAM bounds.

Chapter 09

Parameter-Efficient Tuning (LoRA)

Training a foundational model requires millions of GPU hours. If an enterprise wishes to adapt a model to a specific subject, full fine-tuning is computationally prohibitive. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) solves this via linear algebra matrix decomposition.

Instead of updating a massive weight matrix $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$, LoRA hypothesizes that the "update" necessary for a specific concept resides in a low intrinsic rank. We freeze $W_0$ and represent the weight update $\Delta W$ as the product of two low-rank matrices $A$ and $B$:

$$ W = W_0 + \Delta W = W_0 + BA $$

Where $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$ and $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}$, and the rank $r \ll \min(d, k)$. Typically, $r$ is extremely small (e.g., $r=4$ or $8$). Matrix $A$ is initialized randomly, and $B$ is initialized to zero, ensuring $\Delta W = 0$ at the start of training. This reduces the number of trainable parameters by over 10,000x, allowing models to be fine-tuned on consumer GPUs in minutes.

Chapter 10

Spatial ControlNet & Adapters

While CFG allows semantic text control, developers often require strict spatial structural control (e.g., forcing a generated human into a specific skeletal pose, or preserving the exact edges of an architectural sketch). ControlNet resolves this without requiring retraining of the foundational base model.

ControlNet operates by cloning the weights of the foundational U-Net's encoder. The original encoder is locked (frozen). The cloned encoder is made trainable and receives the spatial condition (e.g., Canny edge map or OpenPose skeleton) alongside the latent $x_t$.

The trainable clone processes the spatial map, and its outputs are injected back into the frozen decoder's skip connections. To prevent the untrained clone from destroying the fragile generative capability of the frozen model during the first training epoch, ControlNet employs Zero-Convolutions—$1 \times 1$ convolutional layers whose weights and biases are explicitly initialized to zero. Because the gradients flow naturally through zero-convs, the network safely learns to interpret the spatial map over time without gradient explosions.

Chapter 11

Diffusion Transformers (DiT)

While U-Nets dominated the early 2020s, scaling them up (to billions of parameters) yielded diminishing returns and complex inductive biases due to localized convolutions. Peebles and Xie (2022) proposed replacing the U-Net entirely with a standard Transformer architecture, creating the DiT (used by Sora, Stable Diffusion 3, and Flux).

The DiT Architecture

DiTs operate on latents by "patchifying" them, identical to a Vision Transformer (ViT). A $32 \times 32$ latent is sliced into $p \times p$ patches, unrolled into a sequence of tokens, and processed by standard Transformer blocks (Self-Attention $\to$ MLP).

Crucial Innovation: AdaLN-Zero

How do we inject the timestep $t$ and class condition $c$ into a Transformer? DiTs utilize Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN). A linear layer projects $(t, c)$ into scale ($\gamma$), shift ($\beta$), and dimensional scaling ($\alpha$) parameters.

h = γ(t,c) * LayerNorm(x) + β(t,c)
x_out = x + α(t,c) * MLP(h)

Crucially, the MLPs predicting $\gamma, \beta, \alpha$ are initialized to zero. This guarantees that at initialization, every DiT block acts as an identity function ($x\_out = x$). This enables the training of monstrously deep Transformers (e.g., 50+ layers) with absolute gradient stability from step one.

Chapter 12

Advanced ODE Solvers (DDIM/DPM)

The native ancestral sampling of DDPM is an incredibly slow Markov chain, requiring $T$ evaluations of the neural network (often 1000). Song et al. noted that for every diffusion SDE, there exists a deterministic Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) called the Probability Flow ODE, whose trajectories share the exact same marginal probability densities.

Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM)

DDIM bypasses the Markovian restriction by defining a non-Markovian forward process with the same marginals. It deterministically links $x_{t-1}$ directly to $x_t$ and the predicted clean image $x_0$. Because the process is deterministic, one can define a sub-sequence of timesteps $\tau \subset [1, \dots, T]$ and calculate steps with large "strides", effectively dropping generation requirements from 1000 steps to 20-50 steps.

DPM-Solver

Because the reverse process is just an ODE, we can employ advanced numerical ODE solvers (like Runge-Kutta). DPM-Solver analytically integrates the linear part of the Diffusion ODE, treating the neural network $\epsilon_\theta$ as the non-linear element to be approximated via Taylor expansions. This enables generation of high-quality samples in as few as 10 to 15 steps.

Chapter 13

Flow Matching & Rectified Flows

While Score-SDEs and DDPMs force the data to diffuse along a fixed, curved Gaussian path defined by the $\beta_t$ schedule, this curvature causes inefficiency during ODE solving. Flow Matching (and the specific variant Rectified Flows) supersedes DDPM by learning a Continuous Normalizing Flow.

Instead of a schedule, we establish an explicit, perfectly straight line transport path between pure noise $x_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ and the true data $x_0 \sim p_{data}$. We define the latent trajectory as a simple interpolation:

$$ X_t = t \cdot x_1 + (1 - t) \cdot x_0, \quad t \in [0,1] $$

The neural network $v_\theta(X_t, t)$ is trained to directly predict the vector field (the velocity derivative):

$$ L = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, x_1} \left[ \Vert (x_1 - x_0) - v_\theta(X_t, t) \Vert^2 \right] $$

Because the transport path is a straight line, solving the ODE during inference requires vastly fewer integration steps (Euler's method works perfectly). This is the underlying mathematics of the most advanced 2024+ models (Stable Diffusion 3, Flux), yielding generation in 4 to 8 steps natively without requiring knowledge-distillation tricks.

Chapter 14

Discrete & Alternative Paradigms

Standard diffusion relies on continuous numbers, making it natively terrible at generating discrete data like text or code tokens. To solve these harder challenges, researchers have developed entirely new paradigms.

Discrete/Multinomial Diffusion

Instead of adding Gaussian noise, the forward process acts as a Markov transition matrix that randomly replaces words with a `[MASK]` token, or mutates them into random other categorical tokens over time. The reverse model (usually a Transformer) is trained to look at the corrupted text and predict the uncorrupted tokens. This bridges the gap between Autoregressive LLMs and Diffusion, allowing diffusion-like generation of language.

Consistency Models

To drop generation speeds from 50 steps down to 1 step, Consistency Models enforce a mathematical constraint: any point on a noisy trajectory must map directly back to the exact same clean origin point ($x_0$). By enforcing this "consistency" across the trajectory during training, the model can look at an image of pure static and jump straight to a beautiful, clean image in a single pass. This unlocks true zero-latency real-time generation.

Chapter 15

Industry Applications

The mathematics of reversing entropy extends far beyond AI art generation.

Audio & Speech Synthesis

Audio waves are represented visually as spectrograms. Diffusion models trained on these 2D tensors generate highly realistic human speech, original music tracks, or entirely new sound effects from text prompts.

Computational Biology

Instead of diffusing pixels, scientists diffuse the 3D coordinates of atoms. Systems like RoseTTAFold Diffusion generate entirely new, functional protein structures, accelerating pharmaceutical drug discovery.

Time-Series Forecasting

Financial analysts use 1D diffusion models to predict market trends. By treating historical data as the "signal" and future uncertainty as "noise," models generate highly probable future scenarios.

Physics-Accurate Video

Treating video as a 4D tensor (height, width, channels, time), diffusion models generate highly coherent video clips that demonstrate emergent understanding of object permanence and lighting.

Chapter 16

State of the Art Frontiers

Diffusion models have moved rapidly from academic theory to industry-dominating production systems. Ongoing experiments are shaping the present day:

  • Adversarial Diffusion Distillation (ADD): By merging adversarial GAN losses with Diffusion objectives, models like SDXL Turbo can generate 1-megapixel images in 1 to 4 steps (under 100 milliseconds), enabling real-time video game rendering.
  • Multimodal Any-to-Any Diffusion: Merging LLMs with Diffusion Models natively. Unified architectures ingest a mixture of text, audio, and images simultaneously, and diffuse a coherent mixture of audio and video out.
  • 3D Gaussian Splatting Diffusion: Instead of diffusing 2D pixels, experimental pipelines diffuse "Gaussian Splats" (a novel method of representing 3D space), allowing the generation of fully navigable, photorealistic 3D environments.
Chapter 17

Advanced PyTorch Architecture

We transition from theory to applied engineering. Below is the architectural PyTorch code for a critical component of modern models: The Diffusion Transformer (DiT) Block utilizing AdaLN-Zero, resolving the injection of timesteps ($t$) into spatial sequence processing.

import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import math

class ModulatedLayerNorm(nn.Module):
    """
    Implements AdaLN (Adaptive Layer Normalization)
    Projects the timestep embedding into shift, scale, and gate parameters.
    """
    def __init__(self, d_model):
        super().__init__()
        self.ln = nn.LayerNorm(d_model, elementwise_affine=False)
        self.mlp = nn.Sequential(
            nn.SiLU(),
            nn.Linear(d_model, 6 * d_model) # Generates 6 vectors for AdaLN
        )

    def forward(self, x, c):
        # c is the temporal/condition embedding
        shift_msa, scale_msa, gate_msa, shift_mlp, scale_mlp, gate_mlp = self.mlp(c).chunk(6, dim=-1)
        
        # Apply normalization and modulate with timestep scale/shift
        x_norm = self.ln(x)
        x_modulated = x_norm * (1 + scale_msa.unsqueeze(1)) + shift_msa.unsqueeze(1)
        return x_modulated, gate_msa, shift_mlp, scale_mlp, gate_mlp


class DiTBlock(nn.Module):
    """
    A single Diffusion Transformer block utilizing AdaLN-Zero.
    """
    def __init__(self, hidden_size, num_heads):
        super().__init__()
        self.norm1 = ModulatedLayerNorm(hidden_size)
        self.attn = nn.MultiheadAttention(hidden_size, num_heads, batch_first=True)
        
        self.ln2 = nn.LayerNorm(hidden_size, elementwise_affine=False)
        self.mlp = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(hidden_size, hidden_size * 4),
            nn.GELU(),
            nn.Linear(hidden_size * 4, hidden_size)
        )
        
        # AdaLN-Zero Initialization
        # Ensures that at initialization, the block is an exact identity function.
        nn.init.constant_(self.norm1.mlp[-1].weight, 0)
        nn.init.constant_(self.norm1.mlp[-1].bias, 0)

    def forward(self, x, c):
        # 1. Modulate inputs using the Time/Condition embedding
        x_mod, gate_msa, shift_mlp, scale_mlp, gate_mlp = self.norm1(x, c)
        
        # 2. Self-Attention with Gated Residual
        attn_out, _ = self.attn(x_mod, x_mod, x_mod)
        x = x + gate_msa.unsqueeze(1) * attn_out
        
        # 3. MLP block with second Modulation & Gated Residual
        x_norm2 = self.ln2(x)
        x_mod2 = x_norm2 * (1 + scale_mlp.unsqueeze(1)) + shift_mlp.unsqueeze(1)
        mlp_out = self.mlp(x_mod2)
        x = x + gate_mlp.unsqueeze(1) * mlp_out
        
        return x

By enforcing nn.init.constant_(..., 0) on the final linear projection of the AdaLN MLP, we guarantee $\gamma = 0, \beta = 0, \alpha = 0$ at step 0 of training. The Transformer acts as a perfect identity pass-through, eliminating gradient vanishing issues in 50+ layer unrolls.
